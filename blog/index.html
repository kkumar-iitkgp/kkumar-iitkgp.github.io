A non-coding deletion linking the gonadal axis to striatal morphology

A small structural interval upstream of TACR3 looks, at first, like a hormone locus. But read through a neuroimaging lens, it becomes more interesting: DRAGEN-WGS, Garg 2026 AJHG, and Big40-Oxford all point toward a coherent bilateral striatal MRI signal. This post focuses on that hidden neuroendocrine–striatal story and what it reveals about structural variation in brain imaging genetics.

July 9, 2026
A 20-year-old brain-size gene, a whole-brain CRISPR atlas, and the cell type nobody was looking at

Two decades ago, Chenn & Walsh showed β-catenin sets cortical size; later work established APC as its Wnt-pathway antagonist. GWAS caught the pathway — but never these two genes individually, and never with a cell type. The first genome-scale, whole-brain, in-vivo Perturb-seq atlas quietly supplies the missing piece, in a structure and life-stage the classic work never reached.

What can brain organoids tell us about CNVs?

Brain organoids offer a controlled window into how genomic dosage alters early human neurodevelopment. Using 16p11.2 and recent cross-mutation organoid studies as examples, this post asks what these models genuinely add to CNV, psychiatric, and neuroimaging genetics—and where their evidence should stop.
